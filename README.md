## What you're actually buying

Hydro Boost Water Gel is a gel, not a cream, and that difference explains most of the love-it-or-leave-it split in the reviews. It's water-based, oil-free, and spreads thin — the texture people describe as sinking in before you've finished your face.

The current formula, as described in Neutrogena's own product copy, is built on a "NMF complex" containing hyaluronic acid (up 20% versus the previous formula), amino acids, and electrolytes, with a claim of up to 72 hours of hydration and a clinically tested "6x more hydration" figure against an untreated control. Those numbers come from the brand's marketing copy rather than independent trials, so treat them as direction, not gospel. The parts that matter practically: hyaluronic acid holds water, the gel format means no occlusive layer, and the jar is 50 ml / 1.7 oz.

Versions worth knowing about before you order:

- **Water Gel (50 ml)** — the standard jar. The one everyone means.
- **Gel-Cream / emulsion** — a richer, creamier formula aimed at dry skin. Different product, similar packaging. This is the single most common mix-up in reviews.
- **15 ml mini** — cheap way to test before committing. Worth it if you're fragrance-sensitive, because the scent is the most divisive thing about it.
- **Fragrance-free variant** — exists in some markets and gets notably better feedback from people with reactive skin, though availability varies and the standard jar is scented in several regions.
- **Multi-packs and refill pods** — a 2-pack showed up at roughly €37 for two jars in one European listing; refill formats exist in at least one market. Multi-buy is usually where the per-jar price drops.

## Who it suits, and who should skip it

The pattern in customer reviews across Amazon UK, Amazon India, Amazon Singapore, eBay, and LookFantastic is consistent enough to describe, and it's not a straight "great product" verdict.

Oily and combination skin is where it earns its reputation. Reviews repeatedly mention a matte finish, fast absorption, no breakout on acne-prone skin, and enough hydration under makeup. One Amazon India reviewer who'd repurchased four times called it the only moisturiser that doesn't feel like it's suffocating their skin in humid weather. Amazon UK's listing sits at **4.5 out of 5 from 23,328 ratings**, and LookFantastic's version holds **4.58 out of 5 from 262 reviews** — both genuinely strong for a drugstore moisturiser.

Dry skin is where it falls down. Reviewers with dry skin say it isn't enough on its own, and Neutrogena's own product line splits along the same line: the water gel is pitched at normal-to-combination, while the cream and emulsion versions are aimed at drier skin. If you're chasing this product because it's popular, and you wake up with tight cheeks, you probably want the cream, not the gel.

Fragrance is the other fault line. On the same product page you'll find "has a lovely scent, only lightly scented" sitting next to "the fragrance is a little too strong" and, in harsher cases, complaints about stinging around the eyes. The French review aggregator INCI Beauty rates this formula **9.8 out of 20**, flagging it for silicones and fragrance in the composition. That's one site's methodology, not a dermatology verdict, but the direction matches what users report: this is a fragrance-forward formula in most markets, and reactive skin does better with the unscented version.

> Short version: oily, combination, and acne-prone skin — yes, with good reason. Dry skin — get the cream. Fragrance-sensitive — find the fragrance-free version or test with the 15 ml mini first.
